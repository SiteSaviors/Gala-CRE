# Gala CRE listing-structure research — source record

Research date: September 4, 2026
Research question: How should Gala CRE structure active commercial-property and land listing pages, based on the supplied first-party developer, owner-operator, investment-manager, and brokerage websites?

## Working conclusion

The reference set contains three materially different digital products:

1. **Developer portfolio / project pages** — prove experience through project scale, role, status, program, imagery, and a concise story.
2. **Active brokerage / leasing inventory** — help a prospect qualify a live opportunity through availability, hard facts, documents, location, and direct contact.
3. **Investment-manager / REIT websites** — communicate strategy, portfolio scale, performance, reporting, and risk; they are not models for public property listings.

Gala's current three routes represent live for-sale opportunities. They should use the second model. A separate transactions or development-project system can later use the first model.

## Source-backed synthesis

### Strong findings

- **Portfolio projects and live listings must be separate content systems.** Beacon publishes a searchable property inventory and a distinct project gallery with different filters and goals.
- **A listing page is a qualification tool.** Beacon and L&L prioritize current availability, physical/property facts, downloadable materials, location, and a named human contact.
- **The data model must vary by asset class.** Land is naturally filtered and summarized by acreage; buildings and spaces by square feet and availability. Beacon implements both in the same inventory without forcing identical fields.
- **Status is critical.** Golub, Bayshore, Trilogy, Thornton, and Pearlstone all surface lifecycle or stage in their portfolio organization.
- **Developer pages prove execution through role, stage, program, and outcome.** Focus names its role; Magellan quantifies the program; Leonard describes the development constraint solved.
- **Marketing assertions require discipline.** Clarion, BAM, Canfield, Beacon, and Crexi pair claims with contextual qualification or disclosure.

### Inferences for Gala

- The current Gala pages are too long because they combine an active listing, a developer project profile, and a marketing landing page.
- A buyer should not have to scroll through several editorial sections to find price, acreage, approved use, flyer, or the listing broker.
- For Gala's small inventory, an elaborate marketplace is unnecessary. A fast filterable index and decisive details are enough.
- As Gala grows, `Property`, `Project`, and `Transaction` should become separate content types, even if they share imagery and card components.

## Evidence gaps

- Full offering memoranda, surveys, site plans, zoning documents, utility confirmations, parcel IDs, tax data, environmental materials, traffic counts, and verified advisor headshot/contact details were not provided in the repository.
- Crexi exposes a flyer action, but the underlying flyer URLs were not available in the crawled listing text.
- The Church Street listing contains an address conflict: its title/description use 611 and 703 Church Street, while the embedded two-location map identifies 611 and 711 Church Street.
- The Church Street listing contains an acreage conflict: the structured detail is 1.000 acre, while the headline and description use approximately 1.09 acres.
- The Lackey Street listing does not make clear whether the parcels described as available behind and west of the store are included in the 0.70-acre, $549,000 offering.
- Lackey's I-95 construction explanation and seller tank-removal language are time-sensitive or legally material and need direct confirmation before reuse.
- Yadkin's “permit-ready” and approval statements need the underlying site plan and municipal approval documents before the Gala site presents them without attribution.
- The supplied list is weighted toward developers and investment firms. Beacon and L&L are the most relevant sources for active-property behavior; Crexi is the most relevant source for Gala's actual listing facts.
- No unverified metric should be added merely because a comparable site uses it.

## Decision record

Recommended content systems:

1. `/properties` — active, under-contract, and closed property inventory.
2. `/properties/:slug` — fact-led listing route with asset-specific fields and direct inquiry.
3. `/projects` or `/development` — future development portfolio using role, stage, program, team, and imagery.
4. `/transactions` or `/case-studies` — future brokerage proof using challenge, representation, actions, and outcome.
